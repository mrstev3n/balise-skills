# Easter egg doctrine

Load this file when the choreography plan reaches its fourth stratum, or when a project asks for personality beyond the expected.

Easter eggs are part of the signature of awarded sites: they reward exploration and signal artisanal care. Treat them as full design elements, planned in the motion map — not gadgets bolted on at the end.

## Recognized patterns

- **Secret key sequences** (konami-style) triggering a visual variant, hidden mode, or scene.
- **Prolonged gestures**: long-press, sustained pointer hold, scrolling past the end of the page.
- **Hidden pages**: 404, maintenance, legal pages, or empty states treated as moments of play.
- **Console art**: message, ASCII art, or recruiting note in the developer console — a classic studio signature.
- **Secret hover zones**: a corner of the logo, a footer pixel that reacts.
- **Seasonal or contextual variants**: date, time, user's weather, nth visit.
- **Playful empty states**: absence of content becomes an occasion for personality.

## Non-negotiable constraints

- An easter egg **never** degrades accessibility, performance, or the main journey: purely additive, disable-able, and carries no functionality away with it.
- No audio autoplay; no keyboard capture that conflicts with navigation or assistive shortcuts.
- Every easter egg is registered in the motion map with its cost (weight, risk) and its exit; the rest of the experience must work fully without it ever being discovered.
- Announce planned easter eggs honestly to the user/client — they are secrets for visitors, not for the client.
