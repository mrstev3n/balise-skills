# Discovery and opportunity finding

## Route A — explore without a corpus

Start with a bounded domain, public, or situation. Define what counts as an in-scope episode and which people, alternatives, constraints, and outcomes matter. Generate research questions before ideas. Seek multiple signal types: observed behavior, workarounds, repeated tasks, failures, costs, constraints, institutional rules, and desired outcomes. Mark analogies and forecasts as hypotheses, not findings.

Useful first outputs are an exploration brief, a signal map, a list of unknowns, and the next low-cost observation or conversation. Do not manufacture personas, pain points, or opportunity size from a thin description.

## Route B — raw corpus without an idea

### 1. Ingest with provenance

Create a source ledger before synthesis:

| Field | Record |
|---|---|
| Source ID | Stable ID for file, interview, event, respondent, dataset, or document |
| Origin | Who/where/when/how it was collected |
| Transformation | Transcription, redaction, translation, OCR, sampling, or extraction |
| Access limit | Missing material, selective access, or another gap that limits what can be concluded |
| Scope | Population, situation, time window, and exclusions |
| Access/ethics | Consent, privacy, sensitivity, and permitted use |

Keep raw material unchanged. Preserve uncertainty introduced by extraction, paraphrase, translation, or selective access.

### 2. Separate layers

Code each useful excerpt or event as one or more of:

- observation: what was seen, said, done, measured, or recorded;
- interpretation: a proposed meaning or pattern;
- hypothesis: what may explain or predict it;
- context/constraint: conditions that alter the meaning;
- contradiction/minority: a case that does not fit the emerging pattern.

Do not promote an interpretation to an insight merely because it sounds compelling. An insight should explain a meaningful relationship or tension and point to an implication, while retaining links to the underlying observations.

### 3. Group without erasing

Cluster by behavior, task, frustration, workaround, constraint, contradiction, desired result, or context. Keep a record of minority cases and actively search for disconfirming cases. Do not claim saturation unless the sampling, coding process, scope, and stopping rule justify it; often the honest status is `not assessed` or `partial coverage`.

Frequency, intensity, reach, consequence, urgency, and strategic importance are different dimensions. Report them separately. A rare severe failure and a frequent mild inconvenience should not be collapsed into one count.

### 4. Move from evidence to opportunity

Use a traceable form:

```text
In [context], [actor] is trying to [task/outcome] but [observed tension/workaround/constraint].
This matters because [consequence supported by sources].
Opportunity candidate: enable [actor] to [outcome] without [important trade-off].
Evidence: [source IDs and excerpts].
Interpretation: [bounded reading of the evidence].
Missing evidence / rival explanation: [list].
```

An opportunity is not automatically a product feature. Generate response families independently: product, service, process, content, policy, education or guidance, automation, an improvement to an existing alternative, or no new solution. Only then derive ideas, hypotheses, and tests.

## Route C — suspected problem

Reconstruct the episode before accepting the label “pain point”: who experienced what, when, with which alternative, workaround, cost, constraint, and outcome? Compare at least two plausible causes where the data allow it. State what would disconfirm the frame and which affected or absent voices still need investigation.

## Anti-cherry-picking checks

- Link every important synthesis to source IDs, not only illustrative quotations.
- Show negative, minority, and ambiguous cases.
- Do not infer a persona from one person or a segment from a convenient network.
- Do not call a request a need, a complaint a pain point, or a stated preference a behavior without qualification.
- Keep the unanswered research questions visible after an opportunity statement.
