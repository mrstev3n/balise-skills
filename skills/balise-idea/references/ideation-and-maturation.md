# Ideation and maturation

## Keep the concepts distinct

| Term | Working meaning | Do not confuse it with |
|---|---|---|
| Idea | A possible response, direction, or proposition | Evidence or a decision |
| Opportunity | A traceable space of potential value or change | A feature or guaranteed market |
| Concept | An idea made concrete enough to discuss | A built solution |
| Value proposition | A hypothesis about who gets what value and why | Proof of desire or payment |
| Solution | A proposed way to address an opportunity | The problem itself |
| Hypothesis | A claim whose uncertainty can be reduced by an observation or experiment | A fact or a vague belief |
| Decision | A chosen next action or stop/hold state given current evidence | A verdict that the idea is true |

## Divergence

Define the boundary, opportunity, or question, and a stopping condition before generating. Produce distinct families rather than cosmetic variants. Useful mechanisms include:

- analogy: transfer a mechanism, then state what does not transfer;
- combination: join elements and explain the new relationship;
- inversion: reverse the goal, sequence, actor, constraint, or ownership;
- abduction: propose competing explanations for an observed result;
- constraint: use a real limitation to force a different mechanism;
- scenario: vary context, time, actor, stakes, or failure mode;
- decomposition/recombination: split a job or system, then recombine selectively.

These are prompts, not guarantees. Mark whether a mechanism is grounded in a source, a theory, a practitioner heuristic, or a Balise IDEA orientation.

During divergence, do not score, rank, or prematurely reject ideas because they are unfamiliar. Keep the source opportunity, assumptions, and difference from existing alternatives visible. If the options are too few or too similar, change the mechanism or family before demanding more quantity.

## Bridge to convergence

Exit divergence when the options are sufficiently different and traceable for the decision, the main uncertainty is explicit, or further generation is producing duplicates. Record an option set containing:

1. the opportunity/problem boundary;
2. distinct ideas or response families;
3. source and assumption links;
4. obvious constraints and dependencies;
5. minority, dissenting, or riskier alternatives;
6. the decision question and missing evidence.

Converge with qualitative criteria chosen for the context: user outcome, evidence of the problem, mechanism fit, feasibility constraints, viability or legitimacy, timing, differentiation, reversibility, harm, and learning value. Explain trade-offs rather than combining them into one universal score. Keep a minority path when it represents a materially different assumption or an important population; stop it only with a stated reason, or test it cheaply.

## Maturation card

```text
Idea/concept:
Opportunity addressed:
Affected actor and context:
Mechanism:
Existing alternatives / analogue:
Important assumptions:
Constraints and dependencies:
What is attractive:
What is uncertain or risky:
What would change our mind:
Cheapest informative next action:
Current status: explore | compare | hold | reframe | test | proceed | stop
```

These are maturation-card statuses, not evidence outcomes or decision states. Here, `test` means that an experiment is ready to run; it does not mean that the test has already been run.

An idea becomes more mature by gaining a clearer mechanism, context, assumptions, evidence, and decision. It does not become more mature by becoming more elaborate or receiving a higher score.
