# Router and decision trace

## Minimum diagnostic

Ask only what changes the route:

1. What material is available now: a domain, raw corpus, suspected problem, idea(s), or an executing initiative?
2. What decision or learning is wanted next?
3. What constraints, affected people, deadline, authorization, and sensitivity matter?
4. What is already known, and what is only assumed?

If one answer is missing but a safe assumption is possible, label the assumption and proceed with a reversible first step. If the missing answer could materially change safety, scope, or external action, ask before proceeding.

## Route selection

| Route | Choose it when | Initial artifact | Exit signal |
|---|---|---|---|
| A — explore | There is a domain, public, or situation but no usable corpus or idea | Exploration brief: boundary, actors, situations, signals to seek, unknowns, research plan | A traceable problem/opportunity candidate or a justified decision to keep observing |
| B — synthesize | There is raw material without a settled idea | Corpus ledger, evidence map, coded signals, minority cases, opportunity statements | One or more opportunity candidates with provenance, confidence limits, and missing evidence |
| C — frame | A problem or need is suspected | Problem frame with affected actors, episodes, alternatives, causes, constraints, and competing explanations | A problem/opportunity hypothesis suitable for research or ideation |
| D — mature | There are one or more ideas | Idea cards, comparison note, assumption map, and next-test choice | A reframed idea, hold/stop decision, or experiment ready to run |
| E — learn | An idea is already being executed | Evidence/decision reconstruction and contradiction log | Deepen, pivot, reframe, hold, stop, or a new learning loop |

Routes may be revisited. Record why the route changed; do not treat the work as a mandatory linear sequence.

## Reusable trace

Keep the trace short enough to maintain:

```text
Trace ID:
Source/provenance:
Route and date:
User goal / decision to inform:
Authorization / accountable owner:
Affected population / sensitivity:
Risk class / vulnerable-population safeguards:
Exposure and version:
Observation(s):
Interpretation(s):
Insight candidate:
Opportunity statement:
Idea/concept/solution (if any):
Hypothesis and uncertainty:
Experiment or research action:
Evidence and scope:
Contradictions / missing cases:
Incident ID / trigger (or `none` with reason):
Containment status / rollback authority:
Reopening condition:
Review label / native IDEA state:
Status:
Decision and owner:
Next reversible action:
Stop / escalation condition:
```

Use one identifier for linked items so a claim can be traced back to the source excerpt, event, observation, or test. A source can support an observation without supporting the interpretation drawn from it.

## Confidence language

Use bounded language: `observed in this corpus`, `reported by`, `plausible interpretation`, `opportunity candidate`, `hypothesis`, `signal`, `supported in this context`, `inconclusive`, or `contradicted by`. Never turn frequency into importance, intensity into prevalence, or a participant quote into decorative validation.
