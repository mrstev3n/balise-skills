# Pattern taxonomy and mechanisms

Use this reference to name a mechanism consistently. Do not treat any taxonomy as exhaustive or as a legal verdict.

## Causal model

Trace each consequential moment through:

`goal → perception → comprehension → available choice → action → consequence → recovery`

Record the product objective separately. Diagnose what the interface does, not what its creator intended.

## Eight exploitative mechanisms

| Mechanism | Observable signals | Diagnostic question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Perception | low contrast, small type, peripheral placement, competing movement, false grouping | Can material information be noticed before action? |
| Comprehension | jargon, double negatives, fragmentation, mental calculation, memory across screens | Can the person explain the choice and consequence in plain terms? |
| Decision | anchoring, defaults, loss framing, unverifiable social proof, artificial scarcity | Does the presentation amplify a bias against an existing preference? |
| Expectations | familiar control with an unexpected result, misleading label, moving target | Does the control do what its label and convention promise? |
| Resource depletion | repetition, delay, interruption, excessive steps, time pressure | Does fatigue or urgency make one branch easier to accept? |
| Forcing | account, data, permission, consent, or sharing required before the primary task | Is the secondary action genuinely necessary for the requested function? |
| Emotion | shame, guilt, fear, regret, social threat | Is refusal made psychologically punitive? |
| Compulsion | autoplay, infinite feed, streak, variable reward, no stopping cue | Can the person see and control time, spend, pause, and completion? |

## Practical pattern vocabulary

The Deceptive Patterns catalogue contained 18 types when consulted on 2026-08-01. Treat the list as a living vocabulary.

| Type | Core signal | Cooperative direction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Addictive Design | loops remove stopping cues or obscure time or spend | add pause, limits, history, completion cues, and explicit control |
| Comparison Prevention | inconsistent units, periods, inclusions, or structure | normalize units, terms, and total payable in one comparison |
| Confirmshaming | refusal is framed as shameful or irresponsible | use direct, neutral option labels |
| Currency Confusion | tokens or credits hide real monetary cost | show the real-currency equivalent and cumulative spend |
| Disguised Ads | promotion imitates content or a native control | distinguish commercial source before interaction |
| Fake Scarcity | unverifiable or fabricated limited supply | show accurate, scoped, dated inventory or remove the claim |
| Fake Social Proof | fabricated or context-free activity or endorsement | show source, period, method, and relevance |
| Fake Urgency | false or unexplained deadline | state a real deadline, reason, and outcome after expiry |
| Forced Action | secondary action blocks the primary goal | defer, make optional, justify necessity, or offer a limited path |
| Hard to Cancel | cancellation is materially harder than signup | provide a direct path from the expected account context |
| Hidden Costs | mandatory charge appears late | disclose total cost before comparison and commitment |
| Hidden Subscription | recurring commitment is obscured | disclose recurrence, first charge, renewal, and cancellation before action |
| Nagging | a refused request repeatedly returns | persist refusal and define a legitimate changed context before asking again |
| Obstruction | refusal, exit, export, deletion, or correction is unnecessarily difficult | remove non-protective steps and preserve a clear route |
| Preselection | a beneficial-to-product option is active by default | require active choice for consequential options |
| Sneaking | product, charge, data use, or condition is introduced without adequate awareness | reveal the material change before commitment |
| Trick Wording | wording exploits ambiguity or double negatives | state the immediate action and consequence directly |
| Visual Interference | salience favors one choice or hides material information | align visibility with importance and consequence |

## Institutional crosswalk

Do not normalize away the source label when attribution matters.

- FTC: induced false beliefs; hidden or delayed material information; unauthorized charges; obscured or subverted privacy choices. Its appendix uses terms including obstruction, sneaking, interface interference, **coerced action**, and asymmetric choice.
- OECD: action forcing, interface interference, nagging, obstruction, sneaking, and social proof among broader commercial-pattern groupings.
- EDPB social-media privacy guidance: **Overloading**, **Skipping**, **Stirring**, **Obstructing**, **Fickle**, and **Left in the dark**.
- Brignull/Mathur practical families: sneaking, urgency, misdirection, social proof, scarcity, obstruction, and forced action.

## Cumulative effects

Inspect combinations across the whole path. A moderate visual preference, emotionally loaded refusal, late disclosure, and added exit steps can compound even when no one element appears severe alone.

Do not claim universal behavioral effect from the presence of a pattern. Record the plausible mechanism and ask for user research or product evidence when actual effect matters.
